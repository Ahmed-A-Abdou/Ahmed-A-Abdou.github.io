Case Study Unity 6 Measurement Extensibility

The Interception

The Problem
Add a completely new threat, an artillery shell from another system, to an air defence built for drones, without editing the defence.
The Plug-In
The C-RAM only knows targets through shared contracts (ITargetable, IHittable). The shell joined through one small add-on component, Interceptable, and one module asset that reports a landing. The defence was untouched, and it engaged the shell the first time it flew.
Measure, Don't Assume
Then I measured it, 16–23 shells per run:
ShotPath past the siteCaught
High angle at the sitestraight down its line of fire100%
Low angle at the sitestraight in100%
High angle, 450 m to the sidecrossing76%
Low angle, 450 m to the sidecrossing67%
Diagnose by Experiment
The obvious suspect was shells slipping between collision checks. Making the physics steps four times finer changed nothing, which ruled it out. The real cause: the defence predicted a straight path for a target that is falling, which barely matters head-on and matters a lot when a shell crosses.
Fix It Once
Each threat can now describe how it flies through an optional IPredictable interface, and the defence refines its lead against that prediction a few times. One justified edit to the defence opened it for every future falling threat: mortars and rockets get the fix for free, and drones, which don't implement it, are unaffected.
The Result
76% → 100%high-angle crossing
67% → 100%low-angle crossing
~300 mfurther from the site

Straight-in shots stayed at 100%, and every intercept moved about 300 m further from the site, because the first bursts now aim where the shell is really going.
