Host NASA Space Apps Challenge Shebeen El-Kom 22 Oct 2026

Build Systems, Not Spaghetti

The Session
I hosted the game development session at NASA Space Apps Challenge, Shebeen El-Kom, a competition I once competed in myself. Fifty minutes for mostly beginners, built around one tunable solar system I made in Unity for the session, so every idea was shown running, not just described.
01 · The Problem
How most of us start: one Update and a flag for every mode (isFlying, isExploding, isWaiting). Nothing stops two flags being true at once, every new mode edits the same method, and teammates collide in one file.
02 · Warm-up: the Rogue Asteroid
One planet, one rock, two ideas. What it is lives in a data asset; what it's doing lives in a state machine with three states (Flying, Impact, Waiting), each a small class that answers what do I do every frame, and when am I done? Live: swapping a big slow rock for a small fast pebble by dragging a different asset onto the same script.
03 · Data Outside the Code
Every planet and moon is a BodyData ScriptableObject: size, orbit, spin, look. Tuning Earth during Play, and the gotcha that changes made to an asset during Play stay after you stop.
04 · Reusable Building Blocks
My open-source Emplacement package: a typed StateMachine<T> (the camera and the asteroid), ObjectPool<T> (the comets), ISpawnable and TransformReference. And interfaces as a promise: the spawner never mentions Comet, only this can be placed.
05 · Swap the Behaviour
The strategy pattern: one question, where is this body on its orbit right now?, answered by a circular orbit and by a Kepler ellipse. Live: writing a new spiral orbit, creating its asset and dragging it onto a planet, with nothing that exists edited.
06 · Composition
Fourteen bodies, one body script, two orbit files, no Planet or Moon class. A moon is a body that orbits a planet; rings are a component plus a data asset; Saturn is a body with rings.
Why It Helps at Space Apps
Tune fast (balancing is changing numbers during Play), work in parallel (teammates in different files), add late (a new feature is one new class), and drop real NASA numbers straight into assets.
New behaviour should arrive as a new asset or a small new class, never as an edit.
The one rule to take home.
