Drone Swarm

One drone, chase camera. About 4 seconds in, its impact point (green) starts to move, and the drone turns after it.

Three V formations, a chase camera behind them, and the C-RAM's tracers coming up to meet them.

The System
One-way attack drones that fly in formation at a defended point, wave after wave. They're the first threat in MilSim and the first thing that announces I'm a target: the air defence engages them through a contract, never by name.
The Drone
DroneController answers three small contracts: ITargetable (position, velocity, alive), ISpawnable (place yourself here) and IHittable (you were hit). It owns a StateMachine<DroneState> with three states: Flying, Intercepted and Leaked. Callers report what happened through intent methods (Intercepted(), ReachedTarget()), and the controller decides what that means; the machine and its states stay private.

Two details that matter: Velocity is exactly the value the drone moved by, so the air defence's lead calculation can't drift out of sync with the flight; and the arrival threshold is larger than one frame of movement, or a drone steps over its target and circles forever.
Following a Moving Target
A drone never stores where it's going. Its target is a TransformReference, an asset the impact point publishes itself into, and FlyingState asks for its current position every frame: am I there yet? If not, aim at it and move. So when the impact point moves, every drone in the air turns after it on the next frame, with no event, no re-targeting code and no reference from the prefab to the scene. The clip above shows exactly that.

The order inside that state matters. It checks for arrival first and returns straight after reporting it, because a state that keeps running after it has handed over is a real class of bug: here it would aim along a near-zero vector and log every frame.
The Swarm
SwarmSpawner<T> where T : Component, ISpawnable: two constraints for two permissions, the pool's gameObject and the Spawn call. DroneSpawner is a one-line subclass with an empty body, because Unity can't serialise an open generic component.

Formations are ScriptableObject strategies (V, diamond, random) that return local-space offsets, so TransformPoint places and aims every formation from one transform. V and diamond were added weeks after the spawner was written, with no change to it.
Waves and the Pool
One wave at a time, not waves on a timer. Overlapping waves would mean the number of live drones depends on how fast the defence kills, which can't be known in advance; one at a time makes pool exhaustion impossible by construction, and OnValidate clamps the swarm size to the pool size so an invalid combination can't be authored.

A bug worth recording: running two spawners produced "pool empty" errors and double returns, because every spawner heard every drone's death on the shared event bus and tried to reclaim drones it didn't own. Fixed with a HashSet<T> ownership check before the return. The counter that starts the next wave tests <= 0, never == 0, so a miscount can't hang the swarm forever.
Role in the Framework
The drone plugs into the C-RAM through ITargetable and IHittable alone. That same contract is what later let an artillery shell become a target without the air defence changing.
